# Test 3 — The Lamina Falsifier (partially run)

**v5.4 §5 attaches the falsifier:** *"Nuclear saturation density is constant across nuclei (~0.16 nucleons/fm³) — a fold plateau. If binding showed no discrete density plateaus anywhere between nucleus and neutron star, 'each fold adds density in thresholds' fails."*

## What is settled

**The nuclear saturation plateau is real.** Nuclear matter saturates at n₀ ≈ 0.16 fm⁻³ (±~0.01), essentially independent of mass number for A ≳ 16. This is textbook and is exactly the signature the construct predicts: a density that stops rising as more nucleons are added. **One plateau confirmed.**

## What is not decidable at present

The falsifier as written asks for **discrete plateaus** *anywhere between nucleus and neutron star*. That region is:

- **Not continuous in principle** — first-order phase transitions (hyperon onset, kaon condensation, hadron→quark deconfinement) would each produce a density jump, which is a plateau signature.
- **But not observed either** — current neutron-star equations of state are consistent with *both* a smooth interpolation and one or more transitions. GW170817 tidal deformability and NICER radius measurements constrain the EoS but do not presently resolve a discrete transition.

So the falsifier is **neither passed nor failed**. It is also, as written, hard to fail: "no discrete plateaus anywhere" is a claim about an entire density range that no single measurement addresses.

## Recommendation: sharpen the falsifier

The construct would be better served by a falsifier that a specific measurement can decide. Two candidates, offered as **proposals, not canon**:

1. **Predict the plateau spacing.** If folds add density in thresholds, the *ratio* between successive plateau densities should be derivable, not free. State it, then test against n₀ and any confirmed neutron-star transition density. A stated ratio that misses is forced-no.
2. **Bind it to a dated observation.** "If the next generation of NICER/gravitational-wave EoS constraints excludes a first-order transition below 4n₀, the threshold form of lamina fails." That is decidable on a timescale.

Absent one of these, the falsifier is attached but not sharp — which is a weaker position than v5.4 §5's wording implies.
