## Scientific Inquiry run
1. Question (precise): Do specialized receptors convert physical stimuli into electrochemical signals, and when does this capacity appear?
2. What an answer must look like: Measured transduction in animal sensory cells, plus evidence on pre-neural transduction.
3. Falsifiability condition: Fails if sensory cells produced no electrical response to adequate stimuli.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measurable: hair-cell latency; microbial photoreceptors; bacterial chemotaxis. Held open: nothing in the core.
5. Test designed: Retrieve primary data on hair-cell transduction and microbial stimulus transduction.
6. Data (unfiltered): Hair cells respond with a 40 µs delay; bacteriorhodopsin converts light into a proton gradient; E. coli chemotaxes. Stimulus transduction exists in prokaryotes before nervous systems.
7. Variable Principle applied: No held-open variable filled.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): Core integrated. Placement note: transduction is not new at Stage 29; what is new is its specialization in cells feeding a nervous system.
9. Documented: This file.
10. Next baseline: Specialized sensory transduction (animal) on top of pre-existing microbial transduction.

## Forcing Test
- Test A (Ground): Measured.
- Test B (Uniqueness): One candidate for the core (transduction occurs).
- Test C (Direction): Consistent.

- **Outcome:** forced-fill (factual core).

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)
Sequence gap: canon places transduction after the Nervous System (Stage 28), but stimulus transduction exists in bacteria and archaea (chemotaxis, microbial rhodopsins). Whether the table should mark the earlier origin is open.

## Narrative stripped
Removed: 'DETECT becomes specialized hardware' (Layer 0 mapping). Reality neither forces nor contradicts the mapping.
