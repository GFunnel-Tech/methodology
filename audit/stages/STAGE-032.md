## Scientific Inquiry run
1. Question (precise): Do neural networks perform pattern recognition, and is interconnection density the cause?
2. What an answer must look like: Recordings of pattern-selective neurons; a manipulation or comparison showing dependence on connection density.
3. Falsifiability condition: The causal clause fails if pattern recognition does not scale with, or depend on, interconnection density.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measurable: pattern-selective neurons. Held open: a measured density→recognition dependence (none retrieved).
5. Test designed: Retrieve primary recordings of pattern/object selectivity.
6. Data (unfiltered): Invariant, sparse object/person coding in human MTL (Quian Quiroga et al. 2005). No fetched study isolates connection density as the cause.
7. Variable Principle applied: The causal claim is held open, not filled.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): Pattern recognition in neural networks: accounted. 'Density produces it': plausible, unmeasured here.
9. Documented: This file.
10. Next baseline: Pattern selectivity measured; density causation open.

## Forcing Test
- Test A (Ground): Measured for pattern recognition.
- Test B (Uniqueness): Causal mechanism not unique (connectivity pattern, plasticity rules and architecture are alternatives to density).
- Test C (Direction): Consistent.
- Test D (Falsifiability): Falsifier: a system with high synaptic density lacking pattern recognition, or low density with it, would weaken the density claim.
- **Outcome:** live-hypothesis (causal clause); pattern recognition accounted.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)
What density, and of what (synapses per neuron? per volume?) — canon gives no measure. A quantitative threshold is open.

## Narrative stripped
Removed: 'emergence' as a framework-level term; reality shows selectivity but does not force the emergence interpretation.
