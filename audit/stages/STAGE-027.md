## Scientific Inquiry run
1. Question (precise): Is a regulated internal variable held within a narrow band across a population, with deviations tracking health outcomes?
2. What an answer must look like: Large-sample measurement of one regulated variable showing a narrow band and individual variation.
3. Falsifiability condition: Fails if a putatively regulated variable showed no bounded distribution.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measurable: body temperature distribution. Held open: generality to all regulated variables (only one fetched).
5. Test designed: Retrieve a large-cohort measurement of human body temperature.
6. Data (unfiltered): Mean 36.6 °C, 95% range 35.7–37.3 °C; individual baselines differ and predict mortality.
7. Variable Principle applied: Only temperature was retrieved; other homeostatic variables are not claimed from memory.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): Core integrated: regulation holds a variable within a band with individual set-points.
9. Documented: This file.
10. Next baseline: Regulated band with individual baselines.

## Forcing Test
- Test A (Ground): Measured.
- Test B (Uniqueness): Consistent with homeostatic regulation; alternative (no regulation) excluded by the narrow band.
- Test C (Direction): Consistent.
- Test D (Falsifiability): Falsifier in step 3.
- **Outcome:** forced-fill (factual core).

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)
'Stasis is death' is not a measured statement: the fetched data show a bounded, nearly constant set-point (a kind of stasis of the variable) maintained by dynamic correction. The phrase conflates constancy of the variable with inactivity of the controller; a precise wording is open for the author.

## Narrative stripped
Removed: 'Garlick Equilibrium organism-wide' (framework construct; reality neither forces nor contradicts it) and the aphorism 'stasis is death; dynamic correction is life' (interpretation).
