## Scientific Inquiry run

1. Question (precise): (a) Is stellar fusion of light into heavier elements measured? (b) Is there evidence that a first generation of stars formed from the primordial (Stage 5) gas?
2. What an answer must look like: (a) direct neutrinos from fusion chains in a star; (b) stars or galaxies at early times, plus metal-poor stars carrying the yield of prior generations.
3. Falsifiability condition: (a) no fusion neutrinos at the predicted rates; (b) metals present in the oldest gas with no stellar source, or no stellar populations before reionization.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measurable: pp and CNO neutrinos; early galaxies to z = 14.32; iron-poor stars to [Fe/H] < -7.1. Derived: reionization half-point z_i = 7.7 +/- 0.7. Held open: the properties (masses, yields, epoch) of the first-generation stars themselves.
5. Test designed: Fetch solar-neutrino results, JWST early-galaxy spectroscopy, extremely metal-poor star abundance, reionization parameters.
6. Data (unfiltered): Fusion in the Sun is directly observed through pp and CNO neutrinos. Stellar-continuum galaxies exist at z = 14.32 ("300 million years after the Big Bang", per the source's wording). The most iron-poor star shows the yield of about one ~60 solar-mass supernova (model comparison, per source). Reionization half-point at z = 7.7. No metal-free star is observed. Elements beyond iron: about half from rapid neutron capture, which is not fusion.
7. Variable Principle applied: Stellar fusion is measured. A first generation is forced by logic (the PDG BBN review lists primordial yields only as D, 3He, 4He, 7Li; metals exist; stars made them; so some stars came first). Its properties are held open.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): The failure to observe a Population III star marks the edge: the first generation is known only through its descendants (iron-poor stars, reionization). Canon's "Heavy elements forged" needs a qualifier: fusion stops at iron (v5.4 §5 agrees); elements beyond iron come mainly from neutron capture (see STAGE-008).
9. Documented: This file. Correspondence: one scale down, the same pp-chain and CNO reactions are measured nuclear cross-sections; one scale up, stellar ejecta seed the next generation (STAGE-008).
10. Next baseline: Further JWST spectroscopy at z > 14 and searches for metal-free stellar populations. Container candidate for stellar fusion (forced-fill, integrated).

## Forcing Test

- Test A (Ground): Grounded: solar neutrinos (two fusion chains), early galaxies, metal-poor stars, reionization.
- Test B (Uniqueness): For fusion as the stellar energy source and element builder up to iron, one candidate survives. For "a first generation existed", one candidate survives by logical necessity.
- Test C (Direction): Forced: light → heavier nuclei up to iron; primordial gas → enriched gas.
- Test D (Falsifiability): Falsifiable (step 3) and survived.
- **Outcome:** forced-fill for stellar nucleosynthesis and the existence of a first generation; the first generation's properties are held open (not graded as filled).

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

Canon's "Heavy elements forged" in Stage 7 does not say which. Fetched data: fusion builds up to iron; about half of the elements beyond iron come from rapid neutron capture (arXiv 1910.10510), whose site includes neutron-star mergers. Gap opened: the stage list has no stage for neutron-capture nucleosynthesis or compact-object mergers (see STAGE-008). Also: reionization (z_i = 7.7) is a measured cosmic transition with no stage of its own.

## Narrative stripped (if any)

- "Per Rhythm: complete pendulum": framework mapping. Reality neither forces nor contradicts it. It does not change the grade.
- "First Stars": the label is observation-compatible (a first generation is logically required), but no first star is observed; the label does not claim one is. No change proposed; the held-open status of Population III properties is recorded above.
