## Canon says

v5.1 Layer I.C, Domain 7:

- *Algorithm:* "DETECT: Supply/demand mismatch. PROCESS: Price mechanism, market clearing, capital allocation. RESPOND: Resource reallocation, approach to next equilibrium."
- *Boom/Bust as Rhythm:* "Markets do not crash randomly. Expansion compresses risk pricing until systemic fragility crosses a threshold. The contraction IS the data that sets the next cycle's causes."
- *2008 Extension:* "Second Kingdom instruments (CDOs, ratings, derivatives) detached from First Kingdom reality. A system running entirely on assumption-filled variables."
- *Variable — held open:* "Whether markets are self-correcting (classical economics) or structurally unstable without intervention (Keynesian/MMT) — held open."

**Split.** *Factual core:* markets register supply/demand mismatches through prices and reallocate; crashes are non-random, preceded by compressed risk pricing and a fragility threshold; 2008 instruments were priced on unmeasured assumptions. *Framework mapping:* Kingdom ordering, Rhythm / Polarity / Yin-Yang, business equivalent.

## Scientific Inquiry run

2. What an answer must look like: (a) experimental or empirical evidence that prices move allocation; (b) a fragility measure with a threshold that predicted crashes out of sample; (c) the primary GAO or Federal Reserve estimate; (d) a named observation that would count as non-mapping.
3. Falsifiability condition: (b) fails if crash timing is not predictable from any pre-crash fragility measure at short horizons while canon claims a deterministic threshold. (d) canon names no failing observation.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: measurable: asset returns, predictability by horizon, laboratory market outcomes. Bounded: short-horizon prices are unpredictable; long-horizon broad course partly predictable. ## Forcing Test
- Test A (Ground): Reallocation via prices has ground (experimental economics). The threshold mechanism has no measurement named.
- Test B (Uniqueness): Several models of crashes compete (random-walk-like short-run behaviour, bubble / fragility models, exogenous shocks). The canonical account is one candidate.
- Test C (Direction): The measured direction (unpredictable short run, partly predictable long run) is compatible with, but does not force, "fragility crosses a threshold".
- Test D (Falsifiability): The threshold claim becomes falsifiable only with a named fragility measure and threshold; canon supplies neither. The mapping names no failing observation.
- **Outcome:** live-hypothesis for the boom/bust mechanism (falsifier attached by this audit: a named fragility index that fails to rise before crashes, or crashes that occur without prior fragility). The self-correcting / unstable variable stays open as canon holds it. Mapping: stipulation.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

- The "price mechanism" is plural: measured outcomes depend on institutional rules. Canon treats PROCESS as one operation.
- "Systemic fragility" and its "threshold" need a measure before the Boom/Bust claim can be tested.
- The $22 trillion figure (Layer I.B) remains unverified by this run; a later run should reach GAO-13-180 or Federal Reserve Financial Accounts data.

## Narrative stripped (if any)

- **"Boom/Bust as Rhythm", "Inflation/deflation: poles of the same monetary axis ... zhongyong"**: Hermetic and Confucian overlays; reality neither forces nor contradicts them.
- **"A system running entirely on assumption-filled variables"** (2008): rhetorical; "entirely" is not measured.
- **Mapping verdict: stipulation.** Mismatch → price adjustment → reallocation is the textbook market description, so the mapping fits. It fits because DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND is general enough to fit any adaptive system; canon names no observation that would count as failure to map. Note that in a market no single agent "detects" the mismatch: prices aggregate dispersed actions. DETECT is a description of the whole, not a step anyone performs.
