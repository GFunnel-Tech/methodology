## Canon says

v5.1 Layer I.C, Domain 1:

- *Three Kingdoms:* "Third Kingdom primary. The spiritual process is the Third Kingdom recognizing itself through the contrast created by immersion in the lower kingdoms. Separation is the necessary precondition for recognition."
- *Algorithm:* "DETECT: Suffering, incompleteness, or the intuition that something foundational is being missed. PROCESS: Deliberate practice — meditation, prayer, contemplation, service, direct inquiry. RESPOND: Integration. Elevated frequency baseline."
- *Cross-Tradition Convergence:* "Buddhist: dukkha → eightfold path → nirvana. Christian: fall → redemption → salvation. Hindu: maya → sadhana → moksha. Sufi: separation → fana → baqa. Kabbalah: exile → teshuvah → devekut. Tao: imbalance → return to the Way → natural harmony. Six independent traditions. Same algorithm."
- The Registry preamble: "if any domain resists the mapping cleanly, it becomes a named variable, not a disproof", and the closing banner: "Ten Domains · One Algorithm · Zero Exceptions."
- The Domain 1 Algorithm (8 steps) adds: "Per Process Density: insight emerges at threshold density of practice, not from a single session."

**Split.** *Factual core (observable):* people report suffering or incompleteness, take up deliberate practices (meditation, prayer, contemplation, service), and some report changed states afterwards; six named traditions each describe a starting condition, a practice, and a goal state. *Metaphysical claims (unobserved):* a "Third Kingdom" exists and recognizes itself; practice raises a "frequency baseline"; the six traditions are *independent* and run the *same* algorithm. *Framework mapping:* the DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND reading, the Fibonacci and Yin/Yang overlays, the business equivalent.

## Scientific Inquiry run
1. Question (precise): Does (a) the observable part of the spiritual process (suffering → deliberate practice → reported change) occur as canon describes, (b) do the six named traditions run one shared algorithm independently, and (c) is the DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND mapping forced by observation or fitted to it?
2. What an answer must look like: (a) controlled evidence that practice changes measurable outcomes; (b) historical evidence of independence plus a comparative method that separates shared structure from shared vocabulary imposed by the comparer; (c) a named observation that would count as the domain *failing* to map.
3. Falsifiability condition: (a) fails if controlled trials show no effect of practice; (b) fails if the traditions share lineage (not independent) or if constructivist comparison shows the goal states differ in content; (c) fails only if canon names an observation that would count as non-mapping. It names none: the Registry converts any resistance into "a named variable, not a disproof".
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: measurable: stress, anxiety, depression outcomes of practice programs. Bounded: whether traditions are historically independent (partly settled by definition: Sufism is within Islam, Kabbalah within Judaism, Christianity arose within Judaism). Held open: the existence of a "Third Kingdom", what "frequency baseline" refers to, whether goal states (nirvana, moksha, baqa, devekut, salvation) are one state.
5. Test designed: Fetch a meta-analysis of randomized trials of meditation with active controls; fetch the scholarly status of the common-core thesis; fetch the historical relation of two of the six traditions to the others.
6. Data (unfiltered): Goyal et al. 2014: meditation programs produce small-to-moderate reductions in negative dimensions of psychological stress; evidence for positive dimensions insufficient. SEP "Mysticism": the common-core thesis (perennialism / essentialism) is opposed by contextualism and constructivism and remains disputed. Sufism and Kabbalah are defined as mystical currents *within* Islam and Judaism (secondary source).
7. Variable Principle applied: The observable part (practice changes some measured outcomes, modestly) is accounted. "Six independent traditions" is not supported as written: at least three of the six (Christian, Sufi, Kabbalah) share an Abrahamic lineage, and the independence of Buddhist and Hindu frames from each other is not established here; held open, not filled. "Same algorithm" is the perennialist thesis, which is contested, not measured. The Third Kingdom and frequency claims have no defined measurement: held open.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): The convergence claim should be downgraded from demonstration ("Same algorithm") to live hypothesis, with independence removed or argued. The six-row table is evidence that a comparer *can* write each tradition in a three-slot form; it is not evidence that the traditions share one structure, because any goal-directed practice fits a start → practice → goal template.
9. Documented: This file.
10. Next baseline: Practice effects: accounted, modest. Convergence: live hypothesis (perennialism). Independence: not supported as stated. Third Kingdom, frequency: unobserved.

## Forcing Test
- Test A (Ground): The observable ground (practice → modest measured change) holds. The metaphysical ground (Third Kingdom self-recognition) has no measurement; it cannot ground a forced result.
- Test B (Uniqueness): DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND is not the only template that fits: any start-state → activity → end-state description of a goal-directed practice fits, including non-spiritual ones (physical training, therapy). The mapping is not unique to this domain.
- Test C (Direction): Direction runs from the template to the traditions: the six rows were written in the template's slots by the author, not derived from each tradition's own structure. Constructivist scholarship (SEP §7) warns exactly against this direction.
- Test D (Falsifiability): For the factual core, falsifiable (controlled trials). For the mapping, canon specifies no observation that would count against it, and the Registry preamble reclassifies any resistance as a variable. As stated, the mapping is not falsifiable.
- **Outcome:** Factual core: accounted for practice effects; the convergence claim is a live hypothesis (falsifier attached by this audit: a constructivist comparison that shows the six goal states differ in content, or evidence that the "same algorithm" reading survives only when the comparer supplies the slots). Metaphysical claims: unobserved-claim, open. Mapping: stipulation.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

- Canon has no procedure for separating a *shared structure* from a *shared template imposed by the comparer*. Without one, cross-tradition convergence cannot count as evidence (Domain 2 Algorithm step 5 treats convergence as evidence).
- "Frequency baseline" is used as if measured. What instrument would read it? Open.
- The spiritual process "resists" the mapping in one specific way: DETECT here is a subjective report, not an observable event. Canon does not name this as a variable, although its own rule requires it.

## Narrative stripped (if any)

- **"Third Kingdom recognizing itself"**, **"Separation is the necessary precondition for recognition"**: metaphysical interpretation; reality neither forces nor contradicts it; no measurement reaches it.
- **"Six independent traditions. Same algorithm."**: "independent" is contradicted by shared lineage for at least the Abrahamic three (secondary source; primary historical sourcing held open); "same algorithm" is the perennialist thesis, contested (SEP). Removed from the factual core.
- **Mapping verdict: stipulation.** DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND is here a descriptive lens. Any goal-directed practice can be fitted to it, and canon names no observation that would show a failure to map. The claim "maps cleanly" is therefore not tested by this domain; it is presupposed. The Registry calls each domain "a test", but a test requires a possible failing result.
- Business equivalent, Yin/Yang, Fibonacci overlays: framework analogies; reality does not force them.
