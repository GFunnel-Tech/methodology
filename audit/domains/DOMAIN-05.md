## Scientific Inquiry run
1. Question (precise): Are canon's statements about Life, Gödel, and the Hermetic dating correct, does the premise used to tighten the Platonism variable hold, and is the DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND mapping forced?
2. What an answer must look like: the rules of Life; the exact statement of the theorem; a dated primary text for the seven Hermetic principles; a named observation that would count as non-mapping.
3. Falsifiability condition: The tightening premise fails if the seven principles, as a set, are first attested after or near quantum mechanics rather than 2,000 years before it. Gödel statement fails if the theorem's conditions differ materially. Life fails if rule count or universality differ.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: measurable: publication data of texts; theorem statements. Bounded: the date of the ancient Hermetica (c. 100–300 CE, secondary). Held open: whether any of the seven principles, as formulated in The Kybalion, appears in the ancient Hermetica; Platonism vs formalism.
5. Test designed: Fetch The Kybalion's text (Project Gutenberg edition), SEP entries on Gödel, cellular automata, and platonism.
6. Data (unfiltered): The Kybalion edition: copyright 1912, originally published by the Yogi Publication Society, Chicago; it lists the seven principles. Secondary: first published 1908; Corpus Hermeticum c. 100–300 CE. Gödel (SEP): conditions are consistency and enough elementary arithmetic; the result is about derivability in a given system. Life (SEP): three cases (birth, survival, death); Turing-universal.
7. Variable Principle applied: Life: accounted. Gödel: accounted in substance ("from within the system" matches derivability-in-F); canon omits the conditions (consistent, effectively axiomatized, contains elementary arithmetic) and "sufficiently complex" is an imprecise stand-in. Hermetic dating: contradicted. The seven principles as a named set are articulated in a 20th-century book (edition copyright 1912; first edition 1908 per canon's own glossary), that is, in the 20th century, not 2,000 years before quantum mechanics. Whether older texts contain them is held open; canon cites no ancient text. The premise that "tightens" the Platonism variable therefore fails, and the variable returns to the range it had before the tightening.
8. ## Forcing Test
- Test A (Ground): Life and Gödel rest on definitions and proofs: grounded. The tightening rests on a dating premise that the fetched text contradicts.
- Test B (Uniqueness): Even with a correct date, "cross-substrate convergence" has other explanations (a 20th-century author writing with knowledge of 19th- and early 20th-century physics and philosophy). The inference to "discovered" is not unique.
- Test C (Direction): The Kybalion is a 20th-century text written in the same era as modern physics; any resemblance may run from contemporary science to the text, not the reverse. Direction unforced.
- Test D (Falsifiability): The dating claim was falsifiable and fails. Platonism itself cannot be settled by measurement.
- **Outcome:** proven-open for the Platonism variable (the tightening is withdrawn). The dating sentence is in conflict; intake `open` because the primary evidence reached is one edition text (measured-single) plus secondary dating, although canon's own glossary already gives 1908. Mapping: stipulation.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

- Canon contradicts itself: the glossary dates the Hermetic principles to The Kybalion (1908); Domain 5 dates them 2,000 years before quantum mechanics. Which ancient text, if any, states them? Open.
- Other places in canon may reuse the "ancient convergence" premise; each needs the same check.
- DETECT in mathematics has no detector: axioms are *chosen*, not detected. Canon does not name this as a variable, although the Registry rule requires it.
