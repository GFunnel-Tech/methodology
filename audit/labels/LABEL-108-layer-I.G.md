## Canon says

v5.1 Layer I.G, subsection "Why The Slime Mold Is The Correct Mechanism":

> "Physarum polycephalum — the yellow slime mold — solves shortest-path problems through mazes that confounded mathematicians until very recently. … Tubes along inefficient paths thin and retract. Tubes along efficient paths thicken. The retraction is not deletion — the protoplasm flows back into the network and redistributes into the surviving tubes. Nothing is lost. … The slime mold does not select the right path. It integrates every path's information into a converged network …"

The layer then generalises: *"Every path is observed. Every path is integrated."*; the Slime Mold Stack marks the mechanism "Confirmed" at quantum, biological, cognitive and civilizational levels; ◇ extended register: *"Slime mold integration applies at every level where complexity emerges — … Confirmed at quantum (Feynman), biological, cognitive, civilizational; predicted at cosmological — Bounded."*

## Scientific Inquiry run

1. Question (precise): What did the Physarum measurements show, and does the label "Slime Mold Integration Principle" (every path integrated, nothing lost) describe that, or generalise beyond it?
2. What an answer must look like: The measured outcome of the primary Physarum studies, and a check of the layer's historical claim about shortest-path problems.
3. Falsifiability condition: The label's grounding fails if the measured behaviour is path selection/pruning rather than information integration, or if the stated history is contradicted by the record.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measurable: minimum-length path found (2000); network cost/efficiency/fault tolerance comparable to Tokyo rail (2010); date of the shortest-path algorithm (1959). Held open: whether retracted material is conserved; whether "information" of failed paths is integrated; the extension to quantum, cognitive, civilizational and cosmological levels.
5. Test designed: Fetch the primary Physarum records and the bibliographic record of the classical shortest-path algorithm; compare with canon's text.
6. Data (unfiltered): Physarum finds the minimum-length path in a maze and builds networks with cost/efficiency/fault tolerance comparable to a rail network. Neither abstract measures conservation of mass or information during retraction. A general shortest-path algorithm was published in 1959, 41 years before the maze paper.
7. Variable Principle applied: Measured: exploration of many paths followed by thickening of efficient tubes and retraction of the rest, ending in a selected short path. Held open: "nothing is lost" and "integrates every path's information". Contradicted: "mazes that confounded mathematicians until very recently" (shortest-path problems had a published general algorithm in 1959).
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): The name should carry what was measured (explore, reinforce, retract) and keep "the Capsule" as canon's coined term. The Tero 2010 result is a real Correspondence point canon did not cite: the same network rule was compared, by measurement, with a city rail network (links to LABEL-103).
9. Documented: This file. Correspondence: one scale up, Tero et al. 2010 is an actual cell-to-city measured comparison (Physarum vs. Tokyo rail). One scale down, tube thickness responds to flow (the model in Tero 2010); not fetched in detail, held open.
10. Next baseline: Proposed for v5.5 (not applied): label "Path Exploration & Retraction Principle (the Capsule)"; strike "mazes that confounded mathematicians until very recently"; cite Nakagaki 2000 and Tero 2010; keep "nothing is lost" as a held-open variable, not a mechanism statement.

## Forcing Test

- Test A (Ground): Grounded for exploration and pruning (Nakagaki 2000, Tero 2010) and for the 1959 date. Not grounded for conservation of retracted mass or information.
- Test B (Uniqueness): For the Physarum behaviour, two readings survive the data: "selection by pruning" and "integration with no loss"; the data fetched do not separate them. Not forced.
- Test C (Direction): The measured outcome is a selected path ("minimum-length solution"), which is closer to selection than canon's "does not select … it integrates".
- Test D (Falsifiability): Canon states a falsifier (a complex system whose emergence cannot be explained by integration of prior path data). A sharper one exists for Physarum: measure protoplasm mass before and after retraction.
- **Outcome:** live-hypothesis for the integration principle. The historical aside is contradicted by a reproduced record (conflict, adjusted).

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

"Nothing is lost" is testable in the organism the layer is named after: total plasmodium mass and tube volume before and after retraction. Canon has not asked for that measurement. The gap: find or run it; if mass is lost (metabolised), the "nothing is lost" reading of the name fails at its own reference case.
