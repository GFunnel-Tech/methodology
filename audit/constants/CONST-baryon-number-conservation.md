## Scientific Inquiry run
1. Question (precise): What does measurement show for this conservation law, and are canon's symmetry pairing and role claims correct?
2. What an answer must look like: Proton-decay limits; any observed B violation; status of 'broken in early universe'.
3. Falsifiability condition: Proton decay observed; or B violation in any process.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measured: proton lifetime limits. Held open: B violation in the early universe (no measurement reaches it).
5. Test designed: PDG 2024 tables.
6. Data (unfiltered): No B violation observed; proton lifetime > 2.4×10³⁴ yr (e⁺π⁰). 'Approximate' is canon's word: B is conserved at every tested level. 'Broken in early universe' is a model inference (a matter excess from symmetric initial conditions would need B violation), not an observation; the excess could instead be an initial condition.
7. Variable Principle applied: Early-universe breaking held open as live-hypothesis, not filled.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): Canon's symmetry column states a model result as fact; it narrows to 'conserved in all measurements; early-universe violation hypothesised'.
9. Documented: this file.
10. Next baseline: Re-audit when Hyper-Kamiokande / DUNE publish first nucleon-decay limits (Phase 9).

## Forcing Test
- Test A (Ground): Measurement for low-energy conservation; model for early universe.
- Test B (Uniqueness): More than one candidate for the matter excess (dynamical B violation vs initial condition).
- Test C (Direction): Measurement → text. Reality is the authority (substrate item 1); the canonical wording is what is tested.
- Test D (Falsifiability): Falsifier: observed proton decay would confirm B violation; none yet.
- **Outcome:** live-hypothesis (early-universe breaking)

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)
Canon's 'why matter exists at all' needs the mechanism of the matter–antimatter asymmetry, which is unmeasured. That mechanism is the gap.

## Narrative stripped (if any)
Removed: "Why matter persists" — conservation of B is one reason protons are stable; reality does not force it as 'the' reason for persistence of matter generally.
