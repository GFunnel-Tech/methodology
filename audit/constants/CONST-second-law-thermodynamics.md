## Canon says
v5.1 Appendix D, *Dynamical Laws*, row Second Law of Thermodynamics, Thermodynamics. **Text:** "Total entropy of an isolated system increases. Per Layer I.E Resolution Two: entropy is local; order is built by throughput. Open systems (life, business, civilization) locally reverse entropy by exporting it."

## Reality shows
| Quantity | Value | Uncertainty | Grade | Source | Retrieved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| entropy-consuming trajectories of a colloidal particle in a translated optical trap (Wang et al., PRL 89, 050601) | 'appreciable and measurable violations … for small systems over short time scales', as predicted by the fluctuation theorem | as published | measured-single | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12144431/ | 2026-09-24 |

The fluctuation theorem makes the second law statistical: entropy decrease has non-zero probability that vanishes exponentially with system size and time.

## Scientific Inquiry run
1. Question (precise): Does the law, as canon states it, match its measured form and domain of validity?
2. What an answer must look like: The precise statement (≥ vs >); the measured status at small scales.
3. Falsifiability condition: Macroscopic isolated system whose entropy decreases.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measured: small-system entropy consumption. Held open: why the universe began at low entropy (the arrow of time).
5. Test designed: Literature fetch (PubMed record of Wang et al. 2002).
6. Data (unfiltered): Canon says entropy 'increases'; the law is ΔS ≥ 0 (constant for reversible processes, at equilibrium). At colloidal scales and short times, entropy-consuming trajectories occur (Wang 2002), exactly as the fluctuation theorem predicts; the law holds on average and for macroscopic systems. 'Open systems locally reverse entropy by exporting it' is correct.
7. Variable Principle applied: The low-entropy initial condition is held open.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): Law survives as a statistical law; canon's wording needs '≥' and the fluctuation caveat.
9. Documented: this file.
10. Next baseline: Re-run on the next PDG edition (2026 Review of Particle Physics) or on the named experiment's next result (Phase 9).
