## Canon says

v5.1 Appendix D (Fundamental Constants & Laws Registry), *Economic, Network & Social Laws*, line 2104:

> **Law:** Wright’s Law / Experience Curve  
> **Statement:** Cost ∝ cumulative production^(-β)  
> **Framework Role:** Each doubling of cumulative production reduces unit cost by a fixed percentage. Per Layer III+: integration density made economic — past production failures inform present production efficiency.

**Split (RUNBOOK rule 4).** *Factual core graded here:* unit cost ∝ cumulative production^(−β); each doubling of cumulative production cuts cost by a fixed percentage.

*Framework mapping (not graded; see Narrative stripped):* 'integration density made economic — past production failures inform present production efficiency.'

## Scientific Inquiry run

1. Question (precise): Does cost fall as a power law of cumulative production?
2. What an answer must look like: Forecast performance across technologies.
3. Falsifiability condition: Wright's law forecasting worse than alternatives.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measurable: cost, cumulative output. Bounded: per-technology β. Held open: none for the form.
5. Test designed: Use the 62-technology test.
6. Data (unfiltered): Wright's law best; Moore's close.
7. Variable Principle applied: Form accepted; β per technology.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): Note β is technology-specific.
9. Documented: This file.
10. Next baseline: Integrated.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

Nagy et al. note Wright's and Moore's laws become nearly indistinguishable when production grows exponentially. Which causes the cost decline (learning or time) is therefore open.

## Narrative stripped

Removed: 'past production failures inform present production efficiency'. The law is a statistical regularity; attributing it to integration of failures is interpretation. Reality does not force it.
