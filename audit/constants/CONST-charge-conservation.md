1. Question (precise): What does measurement show for this conservation law, and are canon's symmetry pairing and role claims correct?
2. What an answer must look like: PDG limits on charge-violating processes.
3. Falsifiability condition: Any charge-violating process observed.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measured: electron lifetime, neutron mode, p/e charge equality. Held open: net charge of the universe.
5. Test designed: PDG 2024 conservation-law summary table and electron listing.
6. Data (unfiltered): No violation observed; electron lifetime > 6.6×10²⁸ yr. Symmetry column: Noether's first theorem pairs charge with the *global* U(1) phase symmetry; the local (gauge) symmetry of electromagnetism implies it too, via the field equations (Noether's second theorem). Canon's 'Gauge symmetry' is acceptable shorthand. 'Perfectly balanced at the universal scale' is a separate claim (net neutrality of the universe), not a consequence of conservation.
7. Variable Principle applied: Universal neutrality held open (bound not retrieved), not filled.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): Law survives; the Polarity sentence is a distinct, un-sourced claim.
9. Documented: this file.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)
Canon conflates conservation (net charge constant) with neutrality (net charge zero). The universe's net charge is a separate quantity; its measured bound is the gap.

## Narrative stripped (if any)
Removed: "Per Polarity: positive and negative are perfectly balanced at the universal scale." Mapping plus an un-sourced cosmological claim; conservation does not force it (a conserved net charge could be non-zero).
