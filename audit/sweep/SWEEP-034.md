## Canon says

v5.1 Layer I.C, Domain 9 — Biological Process, Apoptosis row:

> "Apoptosis | Cells that have served their function self-terminate. A cell that refuses apoptosis becomes cancer. The organizational equivalent requires no further elaboration."

v5.1 Layer I.E, Resolution Four:

> "Stars die, but supernovae seed the next gradient. Cells die, but apoptosis feeds the next cell. ... Per Correspondence: same answer at every scale. Zero exceptions."

v5.1 Cross-Reference Index: "Apoptosis | Layer I.C Domain 9 | II Rhythm, V Pillar IV, I.E | Deliberate dissolution as gradient seeding."

**Split (RUNBOOK rule 4).** *Factual core graded here:* programmed self-termination of cells that have served their function is a mechanism operating at every scale without exception, and the death feeds the next cell.

*Framework mapping (not graded; see Narrative stripped):* "Deliberate dissolution as gradient seeding"; the cancer analogy; "The organizational equivalent requires no further elaboration."

Bacteria are the test case that canon's "zero exceptions" invites. Canon names no bacterial death mechanism; the nearest candidate in reality is the toxin–antitoxin (TA) system, which canon does not mention.

## Scientific Inquiry run

1. Question (precise): Does a canonical item hold bacterial toxin–antitoxin systems, and does canon's apoptosis claim — programmed self-termination at every scale without exception — survive the measured bacterial case?
2. What an answer must look like: canon's apoptosis wording; measured evidence on what TA toxins actually do to a bacterial cell (kill or arrest, reversibly or not); and a statement of TA function as the literature currently holds it.
3. Falsifiability condition: canon's claim fails at bacterial scale if the candidate mechanism is measured to be (i) reversible rather than terminal, (ii) of contested rather than established function, or (iii) directed at invaders rather than at the host cell's own life cycle. **All three hold.**
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: **Measurable** — the arrest phenotype, its reversal by antitoxin, the toxins' differing in vitro activities, DarTG's substrate. **Bounded** — nothing to bound; the finding is categorical. **Held open** — whether a bacterium can be said to have "served its function"; whether lysate-feeding is a designed mechanism.
5. Test designed: identify the mechanism that would have to be bacterial apoptosis for canon's "zero exceptions" to hold, then check the primary literature for whether it kills, whether the death is programmed, and whether the function is established.
6. Data (unfiltered): the table above. The single most damaging row for canon is the reversal row: a state that a cognate gene can fully reverse is not self-termination. The second is DarTG: where a TA system's function *is* established, it is immunity against a virus, which is the opposite of "having served its function".
7. Variable Principle applied: canon fills two variables at once — that a bacterial equivalent of apoptosis exists, and that its function is successor-seeding. Measurement leaves the first as a reversible arrest and the second contested. Neither is filled here. Note also that "having served its function" is not merely unmeasured but arguably unmeasurable for a free-living organism, since it presupposes a superordinate unit assigning the function; that is recorded as held-open rather than resolved.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): canon should restrict the apoptosis claim to multicellular contexts, where it was measured, and should drop "zero exceptions" from Layer I.E's Resolution Four. The failed part contributes something canon can use in exchange: the measured bacterial analogue of "collapse seeds the next gradient" is not death but **reversible dormancy** — a cell that stops rather than dies, and restarts when the gradient returns. That is a stronger mechanism for continuation than apoptosis, and it is measured (SWEEP-033).
9. Documented: this file. Attempted DPR assignment: **DETECT** → decay of the labile antitoxin relative to the stable toxin under stress; **PROCESS** → free toxin acting on its target; **RESPOND** → growth arrest. The assignment fails at the first step for a specific, checkable reason: the "detection" is differential protein turnover, which is a rate difference, not a measurement — nothing compares the two, and no threshold is encoded. It fails at the third step too, because the measured outcome is arrest which is then *undone*, so RESPOND is not an endpoint. And it fails structurally for the case where function is actually known: in DarTG phage defence, the thing detected is the phage and the responder is the phage's DNA, so the arrow leaves the cell entirely. **DPR mapping: does not fit.**
10. Next baseline: an established, reproduced function for a chromosomal TA system in a non-phage context would reopen this. Re-review 2027-09-24.

## Forcing Test

- Test A (Ground): Grounded. The reversibility and the mechanistic heterogeneity are direct in-vivo and in-vitro results; the contested status is stated in the primary literature itself.
- Test B (Uniqueness): Canon's reading is not unique and is not the favoured one. At least three accounts compete for TA function (phage defence, plasmid maintenance/addiction, stress-induced dormancy), and the literature has not selected among them.
- Test C (Direction): The measured direction is against canon: toward reversible arrest and toward anti-invader defence, away from self-termination and successor-seeding.
- Test D (Falsifiability): Falsifiable and failed at bacterial scale.
- **Outcome:** `forced-no` for "same answer at every scale. Zero exceptions" as applied to apoptosis. The apoptosis measurement in metazoan cells is not challenged; its universalisation is ruled out.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

"Zero exceptions" is load-bearing for Correspondence, and it has now failed twice in this sweep — here, and in SWEEP-025 where the Appendix A ordering failed. The gap: canon must decide what a single exception costs. If Correspondence tolerates exceptions it is not a law and cannot support Layer I.B's "proof" language; if it does not, the bacterial death literature falsifies it as written. Canon currently has no rule for handling a failed instance of a claimed exceptionless law, and Layer ◇ does not supply one for this row.

## Narrative stripped (if any)

Removed: "Deliberate dissolution as gradient seeding." Both words fail against the measurement: "deliberate" imports intent no measurement reaches, and "gradient seeding" is unmeasured (held open above). Removed: "A cell that refuses apoptosis becomes cancer. The organizational equivalent requires no further elaboration." Cancer is a multicellular pathology with no bacterial counterpart — an *E. coli* that does not die is simply a growing *E. coli* — and the second sentence declines to make the organisational claim checkable, which places it outside what this audit can grade either way.
