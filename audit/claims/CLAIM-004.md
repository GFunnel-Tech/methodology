## Scientific Inquiry run

1. Question (precise): Has convergence of the two desire types been observed in a recorded sample, and could non-convergence ever be recorded?
2. What an answer must look like: A dataset (n people, protocol, outcome coding) with the fraction showing convergence.
3. Falsifiability condition: Canon's: many people reach genuine extremes and do not converge.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: Measurable in principle: self-reports under a protocol. Held open: everything, since no data are cited.
5. Test designed: Look for the observations behind "consistent convergence observed".
6. Data (unfiltered): None cited. Canon's method (Layer III) states that genuine extremes "must converge", so any observed non-convergence can be reclassified as "not a genuine extreme".
7. Variable Principle applied: Held open. "Consistent convergence observed" is an evidence statement without a record.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): The falsifier and the definition interlock: "genuine extreme" is defined by convergence. The claim is therefore true by definition as written. That is useful to know: it is a stipulation of the method, not an empirical result.
9. Documented: This file.
10. Next baseline: A protocol that defines "genuine extreme" independently of the outcome. Re-review 2027-09-24.

## Forcing Test

- Test A (Ground): None; no observation is cited.
- Test B (Uniqueness): Not applicable without data.
- Test C (Direction): None.
- Test D (Falsifiability): Not operational: the escape clause ("if both are genuine extremes") absorbs every counterexample.
- **Outcome:** stipulation (a method choice, true by its own definition). "Bounded" and "Consistent convergence observed" overstate the evidence.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

To turn this into a testable claim, canon must define "genuine extreme" before observing convergence. The gap: that definition does not exist, and writing it may show that some people's extremes do not converge.
