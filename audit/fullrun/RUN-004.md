## Canon says

Layer I.C's algorithm (v5.1 line 2290), quoted in full:

> Use when: encountering an unfamiliar process or domain and needing to locate it within the framework.
> 1. Identify which of the ten domains the process belongs to: Spiritual, Religious, Psychological, Matrix, Mathematical, Linguistic, Economic, Chemical, Biological, Evolutionary.
> 2. If the process spans multiple domains, identify the primary domain and note secondary domains.
> 3. Map the process's DETECT signature: **what input does it respond to?**
> 4. Map the process's PROCESS signature: what processing architecture handles the input?
> 5. Map the process's RESPOND signature: what output is produced?
> 6. Compare to the canonical algorithm for that domain. Where does the unfamiliar process align? Where does it deviate?
> 7. The deviation is the location of insight — either the unfamiliar process is solving a known problem differently, or it is operating in a sub-domain the canonical map has not yet articulated.

The layer's framing claim: "If Correspondence is law — not analogy — then the Universal Algorithm must be demonstrable in every known process domain **without exception**. This registry is the proof set. Each domain is not an illustration. **It is a test.**" And the closing banner: "DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND · Ten Domains · One Algorithm · **Zero Exceptions**." Canon's escape clause is stated too: "if any domain resists the mapping cleanly, it becomes a named variable, not a disproof."

Domain 9 (Biological Process), the canonical algorithm this run compares against: "DETECT: Internal deviation or external threat. PROCESS: Cellular response — gene expression, protein synthesis, metabolic shift, immune activation. RESPOND: Adapted state, corrected equilibrium." Its Homeostasis row: "The organism continuously monitors deviation, processes the signal through cascading regulatory pathways, and responds to correct."

**Split (RUNBOOK rule 4).** *Factual core graded here:* every process of a bacterium has an occupant for each of DETECT, PROCESS and RESPOND, and Domain 9's canonical signature describes them. *Framework mapping (not graded):* the Three Kingdoms ordering of the ten domains, and the Yin/Yang rows.

## Algorithm run (literal, step by step, subject = a bacterial cell, *E. coli* K-12)

Note on scope. The registry's unit is a *process*, not an organism, so the run is done twice as the algorithm's own wording requires: once on the subject as a whole (steps 1–2), then on two of its concrete processes — **cell division timing** (chosen because it is the organism's best-measured single-cell process) and **chemotactic adaptation** — through steps 3–7.

**Step 1 — "Identify which of the ten domains the process belongs to."**
Produced: Biological (Domain 9). **Completable and unfalsifiable: a bacterium is biological by definition. No observation could have made this come out false.**

**Step 2 — "If the process spans multiple domains, identify the primary domain and note secondary domains."**
Produced: primary Biological (Domain 9); secondary Chemical (Domain 8 — the respiratory chain, H⁺/e⁻ energetics) and Evolutionary (Domain 10 — mutation and horizontal transfer). **Completable. Also unfalsifiable; "spans multiple domains" has no exclusion criterion, and with ten broad domains and a "note secondaries" clause no assignment can be wrong.**

**Step 3 — "Map the process's DETECT signature: what input does it respond to?"**
This is the step that did real work, and it did it by coming up empty in a specific, checkable way.

For **cell division timing**, step 3 demands the input the process responds to — i.e. a molecule that measures the regulated quantity. Measurement says there is none. The adder rule (constant added volume per generation, irrespective of newborn size) reproduces every growth and division distribution in *E. coli* and *B. subtilis* across conditions with **two parameters and no adjustable ones**, is independently reproduced in *E. coli* and the evolutionarily distant *Caulobacter crescentus*, and the second paper states it as "direct experimental evidence **disproving the critical size paradigm**". The rate-limiting component is FtsZ accumulation (treadmilling at ~30 nm/s), implemented "at or close to division". Nothing in the mechanism reads cell size and compares it to a threshold. **The statement step 3 forced: in *E. coli*, division timing is produced without any detection of the quantity it regulates.** I would not have written that sentence unprompted — the natural description is "cells divide when they have grown enough", which is precisely the critical-size model the data disprove.

For **chemotactic adaptation**, by contrast, step 3 has a clean occupant: ligand occupancy of the Tar periplasmic domain is an independently observable input, and two-component systems generally have a *physically separate* input domain in the same polypeptide (≥62 ORFs; "an intrinsic modularity that separates signal input, phosphotransfer, and output response"). **So step 3 discriminates: it is satisfied in signal transduction and unoccupied in size control, overflow metabolism (a fixed allocation rule with three cost parameters, no growth-rate sensor) and translational capacity control (the active-ribosome fraction falls; nothing measures it).**

**Step 4 — "Map the process's PROCESS signature: what processing architecture handles the input?"**
Produced for both. Division: FtsZ accumulation to a rate-limiting level, then treadmilling-guided septal synthesis. Chemotaxis: the receptor-array modification state shifting the active/inactive equilibrium of coupled CheA. **Completable and correct; content supplied entirely by microbiology. For division the "architecture" processes no input, so the slot is filled by relabelling the mechanism as its own processor.**

**Step 5 — "Map the process's RESPOND signature: what output is produced?"**
Produced: constriction and division (division size CV ~10%, generation time CV 40–60%); and CheY-P-driven motor bias. **Completable, correct, trivial — any description of a process names its output.**

**Step 6 — "Compare to the canonical algorithm for that domain. Where does the process align? Where does it deviate?"**
Produced, with four measured deviations from Domain 9's canonical signature ("DETECT: internal deviation or external threat. PROCESS: gene expression, protein synthesis, metabolic shift, immune activation. RESPOND: adapted state, corrected equilibrium"):
1. **No deviation to detect.** Domain 9's DETECT is "internal deviation"; the adder has no deviation signal and no set-point to deviate from. Same for overflow metabolism and active-ribosome fraction.
2. **No transcription.** Domain 9's PROCESS content is transcription-centric. Chemotactic adaptation involves no transcription at any point — it is covalent methylation of an existing protein, and cheRcheB cells show no adaptation over 12 s.
3. **Rate, not level.** Domain 9's Homeostasis row says the organism "monitors deviation… and responds to correct". Chemotaxis regulates the *rate of change* of receptor occupancy; adapted dose-response curves collapse across a 50-fold ambient range.
4. **No apoptosis counterpart.** Domain 9 lists apoptosis as a canonical row. The bacterial candidate is measured reversible (HipA dormancy fully reversed by HipB), its toxins share no mechanism, and the one established function points outward at phage DNA.
**Step 6 did real work: it produced four specific, checkable misfits rather than a verdict.**

**Step 7 — "The deviation is the location of insight — either the process is solving a known problem differently, or it is operating in a sub-domain the canonical map has not yet articulated."**
Produced, and the disjunction resolves to the second branch twice: (a) **detector-free control** — regulation of a quantity with no sensor for it, occurring in at least three measured *E. coli* behaviours; and (b) **post-translational covalent signalling without transcription**, the dominant fast-signalling mode in the domain of life holding most of the biomass (SWEEP-001 reached the same conclusion from the other direction). Both are sub-domains the registry has not articulated. **Result: completable and productive — with the honest limit that every "insight" located is a gap in canon, not a fact about the bacterium that was previously unknown. The registry works as a checklist against itself.**

## Forcing Test
- Test A (Ground): grounded. Genome length and gene count fetched from NCBI this run; all other values reused from `audit/sweep/` files with their original primary sources named. The forced statement rests on two independent laboratories and three species.
- Test B (Uniqueness): unique for the forced statement. Given the adder's two-parameter fit with no adjustable parameters across conditions and species, and the explicit disproof of the critical-size paradigm, there is no surviving reading in which division timing responds to a measurement of size. Not unique for steps 1, 2, 4 and 5, where several assignments fit equally.
- Test C (Direction): here, for once, the directions agree usefully. Canon runs from the algorithm to the mechanism and demands an occupant per slot; measurement runs from the molecule outward and reports no occupant. The demand is what makes the absence visible. That is the whole of the `works-forced` grade, and it is worth noting that the same demand convicts canon's loop: what the layer forced is a statement about the bacterium *and* an exception to the layer's own universality.
- Test D (Falsifiability): the condition set at inquiry step 3 was met. A measured size sensor or a confirmed critical-size threshold would have falsified the forced statement; the field looked for exactly that and the single-cell data disproved it. Steps 1, 2 and 5 could not have been falsified by any observation and are vacuous as applied; steps 6 and 7 are falsifiable only against canon's text, not against the organism.
- **Outcome:** `stipulation` for the registry's framing claim ("each domain is a test… zero exceptions") — the ten-domain assignment is an operator's filing choice with no exclusion criterion, and three empty DETECT slots in one organism are exceptions, which canon's escape clause converts into named variables rather than tests. `utility: works-forced`, on the strength of **step 3 alone**: it forced the statement *"in E. coli, division timing is produced without any detection of the quantity it regulates"*, which two independent laboratories confirm in three species and which contradicts the description any competent unprompted account would give. `diff_state: methodology-gap`, diagnosis `not-reached`: reality shows control without detection and the registry has not grown a slot for it. `intake_result: open`.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)
1. **Control without detection has no home in the registry, and canon's loop has no legal output for it.** Writing "cell size" into the DETECT slot would be a false positive the field has already rejected; leaving the slot empty breaks the loop. Canon must choose, and neither option is currently in v5.1–v5.4.
2. **Step 3's success is a two-edged result.** The same instruction that produced this run's one forced statement also produced three exceptions to "Zero Exceptions", in one organism, all in canon's own strongest domain. F-064's question — is the loop a lens or a law? — is now reached from the registry's own algorithm, not just from the sweep.
3. **The domains have no exclusion criterion.** With ten broad domains plus "note secondary domains", step 1 cannot return a wrong answer. A registry whose filing step cannot err is a filing system, not a test, whatever the layer's framing says.
4. **Steps 6 and 7 audit canon, not reality.** Every deviation located was established microbiology; the novelty was all on canon's side. That is genuinely useful — it is how this audit found its material — but it is not the framework producing knowledge, and the cost register (`audit/cost/`) should keep scoring it at zero.
5. Held open: whether detector-free control generalises beyond the three *E. coli* cases found here. Bounded below at three; upper bound unknown; a directed search across other organisms is the next test, and a reported bacterial size sensor would retract this file's grade.
