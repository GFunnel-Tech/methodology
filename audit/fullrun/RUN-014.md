## Algorithm run (Layer VI, literal, step by step)

**Subject: a bacterial cell (*E. coli* K-12), building a response to DNA damage — canon's own kind of target ("building, fixing, or extending any system… anywhere in any domain"). Step 07 is additionally tested against the measured division cycle, because that is the cycle canon assigns a cellular mechanism to.**

**Step 1 — Direct. "Define the intent before anything else is touched… Inputs: Nothing yet."** **BLOCKED: no measured molecular event precedes detection, and canon's own Layer 0 says so.** The first event of the SOS response is RecA binding single-stranded DNA and promoting LexA autocleavage — detection. There is no prior step in which scope is set. Canon's Layer 0 is `DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND`; Layer VI puts a step *before* detection whose input is "nothing yet". At cellular scale those two orderings cannot both hold, and canon nowhere reconciles them. This is an internal conflict in canon, surfaced by running the layer literally rather than a defect of the cell.

**Step 2 — Guide. "Build the container before information enters it."** **PARTIALLY RAN, and this is the layer's best structural hit.** The sensor exists before the signal: at least 62 two-component ORFs are standing in the genome with "an intrinsic modularity that separates signal input, phosphotransfer, and output response". Canon's step and the measured architecture agree, and they agree on the *non-obvious* part — that input, processing and output are separate physical domains, built in advance.
*Adversarial check.* What observation would have made this false? A mechanism that builds its detector only after the signal arrives. Measurement supplies a partial one: the SOS response, once triggered, *upregulates the repair capacity it then uses*, and inducible operons build the bulk of their machinery after detection, on a small pre-existing basal amount. So the honest reading is: a minimal container precedes the information; most of the container is built afterwards. Canon's step is half-confirmed and half-contradicted, and canon's Failure Mode for the step ("re-organizing messy data is 5x more expensive") has no measured counterpart here at all.

**Step 3 — Gather. "Collect everything. Hold every variable open. Filter nothing."** **RAN and is contradicted, at five independent layers of the same organism.** (i) Restriction–modification destroys unmodified incoming DNA, about 1e4-fold in the comparator measurement — a filter at the boundary. (ii) DNA that enters and fails to recombine is degraded by RecBCD. (iii) Replication discards wrong nucleotides at 1.6e8–1.6e11-fold serial discrimination; fidelity is *bought* by discarding. (iv) Excision repair destroys the item it gathered: the 13-mer it removes is unwound and exonucleolysed. (v) Sensory intake is a high-pass filter — the cell keeps the difference between the past 1 s and the previous 3 s and throws the absolute level away, so hard that dose-response curves collapse across a 50-fold ambient range. "Filter nothing" is `forced-no` in this organism, and the filters are not failures: they are what the cell spends its accuracy budget on.

**Step 4 — Organize. "Structure it into a usable blueprint… map dependencies… socks before shoes."** **BLOCKED in part: the step presupposes an organising agent, and the measured organiser is not one.** The dominant organiser of the *E. coli* interior is macromolecular crowding at 0.3–0.4 g/ml — excluded volume, a thermodynamic constraint with no map and no categoriser — which partitions the cytosol into nucleoid and ribosome-rich liquid-like phases. A chromosome a thousand times longer than the cell is held in about half the cytosolic volume by that, not by a blueprint. Whether bacterial assembly pathways are dependency-ordered in canon's socks-before-shoes sense is **held open**: no assembly-order source was fetched this run, so the strongest possible defence of Step 04 is neither claimed nor denied here.

**Step 5 — Create. "Now and only now does creation begin," and skipping 01–04 means "the build is fast but wrong."** **RAN and is contradicted, quantitatively and in the direction that matters.** In *E. coli*, creation and gathering are the same interval: the ribosome translates a transcript that does not yet exist in full, and the *ribosome sets the polymerase's speed* — accelerate or decelerate the ribosome and RNA polymerase follows; genes rich in rare codons are transcribed more slowly. Rather than the build being "fast but wrong", the coupling is what makes it fast and right: the ribosome prevents polymerase backtracking. Correction then happens *after* creation, not before it — tmRNA rescue terminates about 0.4 percent of all syntheses and degrades the product, and about 85 proteins cannot reach their native state without GroEL. Canon's sequence is a real engineering discipline for systems where rework is expensive and information is cheap. This cell is the opposite case, and canon's step carries no clause for it.

**Step 6 — Database. "If it is not written, it does not exist as a process."** **RAN; half confirmed, half contradicted.** Confirmed: the process is written, in 4,641,652 bp, and CRISPR adaptation writes new experience into the array — canon's step is correct about the primacy of the record.
Contradicted: measured heritable process exists that is *not* written. Persistence is a reversible dormant state passed on in molecule counts rather than sequence, at about 1 in 1e6, tunable to about 10 percent by one allele, and fully reversible by expressing the antitoxin. It is a process, it is inherited, and it is nowhere in the text. Canon's sentence, read strictly, denies its existence.

**Step 7 — Repetition. "The cycle closes and restarts at a higher baseline… each cycle is informed by the two before it."** **RAN and is contradicted with numbers — the sharpest single result in this file.** The measured *E. coli* and *B. subtilis* division cycle is an adder: cells add a constant volume each generation *irrespective of their newborn size*, and two parameters with no adjustable parameters reproduce every distribution in both species in all conditions. That is a return-to-mean rule, not a rising baseline. Worse for the step, lineage memory runs the wrong way: the mother–daughter generation-time correlation is **−1/4** (negative), and autocorrelations of birth size, division size and generation time **decay by a factor 2 each generation**. Canon's step requires memory of depth two with growing amplitude (Fibonacci); measurement shows memory of decaying amplitude and negative sign at depth one. Canon's own cellular mechanism for this step is the Hayflick limit, which F-076 already shows was generalised from one taxon and has no bacterial counterpart; the adder does not restore it, because it bounds size, not lineage.
*New Thought Principle* ("any new input re-enters at Gather, not at the beginning") fares better: after UV, further damage during the response produces additional discrete recA peaks — up to three — rather than restarting the response, and peak count rather than amplitude carries the dose. That is a genuine, if modest, match.

## Forcing Test

- Test A (Ground): Is it grounded? Yes, in observed build practice, and Step 02 is additionally grounded in measured molecular architecture (62 domain-separated sensors standing before any signal).
- Test B (Uniqueness): Does measurement leave one candidate ordering? No. Measurement supplies a second, fully functional ordering in the same organism: detect, build concurrently, filter hard at intake, correct after the build, return to the mean. Two viable orderings means canon's is not forced.
- Test C (Direction): Does it point one way? Yes, and usefully: every step names a real activity, and the New Thought Principle correctly predicts that new input during a response does not restart it.
- Test D (Falsifiability): Is an attached observation available that would refute it? Yes for five of the six factual claims, and four of those observations exist: intake filtering at five layers, concurrent build with reversed causality, unwritten heritable process, and a constant-addition rule with 2-fold-decaying and negatively signed lineage memory.
- **Outcome:** `forced-no` for the seven-step *ordering* as a claim about any system at any scale. The seven *activities* all occur in the cell; what measurement rules out is the sequence and the exclusivity ("now and only now", "filter nothing", "higher baseline"). Step 02 survives and should be strengthened; the New Thought Principle survives.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

1. **Canon has no concurrency clause.** Steps 03–05 assume a serial pipeline. The measured cell runs gather and create in the same interval with the downstream step controlling the upstream rate. This is the same architectural hole as F-075 (canon must state which of its stages are ordered in time, which in logical dependency, and which only in exposition) — now shown inside a single layer, not just in Appendix A.
2. **Layer VI Step 01 and Layer 0 give incompatible first operations.** "Inputs: nothing yet" precedes detection; `DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND` does not. Canon must say which is the base ordering and what the other one is.
3. **"Filter nothing" contradicts canon's own fidelity story.** Canon praises the discarding architecture elsewhere (the dynamic middle, the optimal mutation rate) while Step 03 forbids filtering. This is F-068's collision (Layer I.D's optimum over discard versus Layer I.G's prohibition on discard) reappearing in the operational layer.
4. **"If it isn't written, it isn't real" has a measured exception and no exception clause.** Inherited unwritten process exists (persistence). Either the sentence acquires "written, or held in a stable dynamical state", or it is restricted to organizations.
5. **Step 07 now has no cellular mechanism at all.** Hayflick is restricted (F-076) and the adder contradicts the rising baseline. Canon should either drop "higher baseline" as universal or name the scale at which it starts holding.
6. **No crossover condition is stated between the two orderings.** This is the most useful gap the run opens: canon's ordering and the cell's ordering are both correct in their own regime, and canon has no rule for deciding which regime a system is in.
