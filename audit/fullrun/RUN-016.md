## Canon says

**The instrument (v5.1, Per-Layer Algorithms, "Layer ◇ — The Falsifiability Algorithm").** *"Use when: validating any claim, framework, decision, or assumption."*

1. State the claim with maximum precision.
2. Identify what observation would falsify the claim. **If no observation would falsify it, the claim is not a claim — it is a belief.**
3. Identify the current evidence supporting the claim.
4. Identify the current evidence challenging the claim.
5. Locate the variable boundaries. What range does the claim operate within?
6. Status the claim. Resolved (with evidence), bounded (range narrowed), held open (variable acknowledged), or refuted (falsifying evidence found).
7. If held open: name the conditions under which it would tighten or resolve. If refuted: revise.

**The register the instrument is supposed to have produced** (v5.1, Falsifiability Register): *"A framework that cannot be disproved by any conceivable evidence is not a framework — it is a belief."* Rows tested here, quoted:

- **Process Density** — *"biological systems highest | If a non-biological system were found with greater organized, interdependent complexity per unit volume than a biological cell. | No known counterexample… | Open."*
- **Universal Algorithm (3 operations)** — *"If a process domain were found where DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND is entirely absent. | Ten domains mapped. Zero exceptions. | Bounded. High confidence."*
- **Stage 23 (Appendix A), the claim actually about a cell** — ATP as the *"universal currency"* for molecular machines. (Not a register row; a first-order canonical claim about cellular machinery, included so the instrument is tested on a sharp claim as well as two vague ones. A badly-written register is not evidence of a badly-designed algorithm — that is the point of this run.)

**Split.** *Factual core:* the 7-step algorithm, executed literally on canonical claims about a bacterium, either does or does not produce a falsifier an experiment could fire. *Framework mapping:* ◇'s self-description as *"the Omni Process naming its Gödelian limits explicitly"* and (v5.3 §1) as the Container's falsifiability component whose *"criterion = Test D"*.

## Falsifiability Algorithm run (Layer ◇, 7 steps) — executed three times

### Run 1 — Claim: "ATP is the universal currency for molecular machines in a cell" (v5.1 Appendix A, Stage 23)

1. **Claim, maximum precision:** every molecular machine in a bacterial cell is powered by hydrolysis of ATP (or an equivalent nucleoside triphosphate), so for each such machine the rate of work scales with the ATP supply and not with any other free-energy source.
2. **Falsifying observation (operational, stated before looking):** exhibit one molecular machine whose output rate varies with a *non*-nucleotide free-energy source while ATP is unchanged, and whose force-generating subunits are not ATPases. **This is an experiment: hold ATP constant, vary the protonmotive force, measure speed.**
3. **Supporting evidence:** the great majority of E. coli machines measured — polymerases, chaperones, ABC importers, the proteasome-like AAA+ unfoldases — are ATPases.
4. **Challenging evidence:** the flagellar motor. Speed is proportional to protonmotive force across 0–270 Hz and the stators are ion translocators (row 4). The falsifier of step 2 **fires**.
5. **Variable boundaries:** the claim holds for the nucleotide-driven class and fails for the ion-motive class. Range: "universal" → "the dominant of at least two currencies". Boundary located empirically: ATP-driven **or** ion-motive-force-driven, per machine.
6. **Status: refuted as worded.** Not "bounded" — the word *universal* is what fails.
7. **Revision (step 7 is mandatory, so the algorithm produces one):** Stage 23 should read *"ATP or an ion-motive force"* as a disjunction. Note that the audit's earlier, stronger correction — *replace* ATP with gradients — was itself tested under seal and lost: an archaeal motor is ATP-driven ([PRED-002](../prediction/PRED-002.md), F-084). The disjunction is what survives both tests.

**Verdict on run 1: the algorithm worked.** Step 2 produced a real experiment, step 4 fired it, step 6 returned `refuted`, step 7 forced a revision that a sealed prediction later constrained further. Nothing in this run was a relabel.

### Run 2 — Claim: "No non-biological system has greater organized, interdependent complexity per unit volume than a biological cell" (◇, Process Density row)

1. **Claim, maximum precision:** *cannot be completed.* The claim has the form `Q(cell) > Q(x)` for all non-biological `x`, but `Q` — "organized, interdependent complexity per unit volume" — has no unit, no measurement procedure, and no stated threshold. Best available precision: "we judge cells the densest thing we know", which is not a quantity.
2. **Falsifying observation:** **none can be named.** To fire a falsifier a candidate system must be *measured* on `Q`, and `Q` cannot be measured. Candidate metrics exist and point in different directions: macromolecule concentration in E. coli cytoplasm is 0.3–0.4 g/ml, which a dried protein pellet exceeds; distinct components per unit volume favours the cell; "interdependence" has no metric at all. **By the algorithm's own step 2 — "if no observation would falsify it, the claim is not a claim, it is a belief" — this row is a belief.** That is canon's instrument returning a verdict on canon's own register, and it is the sharpest thing in this run.
3. **Supporting evidence:** canon offers *"No known counterexample"*. Absence of a counterexample to an unmeasurable comparison is not evidence; nothing could have been a counterexample.
4. **Challenging evidence:** none can exist, for the same reason. Note the asymmetry: canon lists step 3 as support and step 4 as empty, and reads that as confidence. The algorithm, run properly, reads the *pair* as the signature of a non-claim.
5. **Variable boundaries:** unbounded in both directions. The claim operates within no range because it names no axis.
6. **Status: not "Open" as canon files it — the algorithm returns "not a claim".** Canon's status vocabulary at step 6 (resolved / bounded / held open / refuted) has **no slot for this outcome**, so canon's own register was forced to file a non-claim as "Open", where it is indistinguishable from an honestly held-open variable. That is a defect in the *vocabulary at step 6*, not in step 2.
7. **Conditions to tighten:** state `Q` as a ratio of two measured quantities (e.g. distinct interacting species per µm³, with "interacting" defined as a measured perturbation dependence), then name the value above which the claim fails. Until then no experiment addresses it.

**Verdict on run 2: the algorithm worked and the register did not.** Step 2 is exactly the diagnostic that F-042/F-043 reached by other means; the algorithm reaches it in one step.

### Run 3 — Claim: "No process domain exists where DETECT → PROCESS → RESPOND is entirely absent" (◇, Universal Algorithm row)

1. **Claim, maximum precision:** for every process domain, all three operations are present. Falsifier as canon words it: a domain where the triple is *entirely* absent.
2. **Falsifying observation:** as worded — a process with none of detection, processing or response. **Nothing measurable can satisfy this**: any system that does anything at all can be described as responding to something. As worded, the falsifier is unfireable.
3. **Supporting evidence:** *"Ten domains mapped. Zero exceptions."* Those ten are canon's own selection ([F-081](../FINDINGS.md)).
4. **Challenging evidence:** MscS is gated directly by bilayer tension; the purified protein in a bare bilayer suffices, and the same area change that registers the stimulus *is* the response. Two operations, not three ([SWEEP-008](../sweep/SWEEP-008.md), F-065). This is a measured partial exception that canon's falsifier, as worded, does not admit.
5. **Variable boundaries — this is where the algorithm earns its keep.** Step 5 asks what *range* the claim operates within, and the answer locates the escape hatch precisely: the claim is stated at the boundary "all three present" but its falsifier is stated at the boundary "none present". The two are not complements. Everything between — one operation, two operations, three operations that are the same event — is unreachable by the falsifier. The gap between claim boundary and falsifier boundary is the entire content of the dispute.
6. **Status: bounded, but bounded vacuously.** The range is narrowed only against total absence, which no physical process exhibits.
7. **Conditions to tighten:** restate the falsifier at the claim's own boundary — *"a process domain in which the three operations cannot be separately perturbed"* — and MscS becomes a live test with a published answer. One word change converts an unfireable falsifier into a fired one.

## Scientific Inquiry run
1. Question (precise): when the Layer ◇ Falsifiability Algorithm is executed literally, step by step, on canonical claims about a bacterial cell, does it produce falsifiers that a named experiment could fire?
2. What an answer must look like: for each claim, the step-2 output written as an experiment (what is held constant, what is varied, what is measured), and whether step 4 or the literature fires it. A pass is at least one fired falsifier and at least one correctly-diagnosed non-claim.
3. Falsifiability condition: the instrument fails if, run on a sharp canonical claim about a cell, step 2 cannot produce an experiment; or if step 6 returns "bounded" for a claim measurement contradicts. Either would show the defect is in the algorithm, not in the register.
4. Variables: measurable / bounded / held open: measurable — genome length, gene count and unannotated fraction, cytoplasmic macromolecule concentration, motor speed versus pmf, MscS midpoint tension and sufficiency. Bounded — the revision to Stage 23 (a disjunction, constrained by PRED-002). Held open — canon's `Q` for process density, and whether PROCESS is a necessary operation (carried to [RUN-017](RUN-017.md)).
5. Test designed: run the 7 steps three times, on one sharp canonical claim about cellular machinery and two register rows, with the step-2 falsifier written down before the challenging evidence at step 4 is consulted.
6. Data (unfiltered): run 1 → `refuted`, falsifier fired by a `measured-reproduced` observation, revision produced. Run 2 → step 2 returns "no observation would falsify"; the claim is a belief by canon's own sentence; canon's step-6 vocabulary has no slot for that verdict and mis-files it as "Open". Run 3 → step 2 unfireable as worded; step 5 locates the mismatch between the claim's boundary and the falsifier's boundary; a one-word restatement makes it fireable and the published MscS result fires it.
7. Variable Principle applied: **Measured** — the five bacterial quantities in `## Reality shows`. **Structurally derived** — the disjunction at Stage 23; the diagnosis that canon's step-6 vocabulary is incomplete. **Held open** — `Q` (unfilled, and not filled here by picking a convenient proxy: macromolecule concentration is a *candidate*, not canon's quantity); the necessity of PROCESS.
8. Model update (Capsule: what the failed parts contribute): the two failed runs are more informative than the passing one. They show the failure is located in **canon's register rows and in step 6's status vocabulary**, not in steps 1–5 and 7. Three of six prior audit findings about non-operational falsifiers (F-042, F-043, F-085) are re-derived here in single steps, which is evidence the instrument is sound and was never run on its own register.
9. Documented: this file; cross-references F-042, F-043, F-065, F-081, F-085, F-084/PRED-002, CLAIM-003, SWEEP-003, SWEEP-008, SWEEP-031.
10. Next baseline: the proposed additions — a fifth step-6 status (`not-a-claim`, for a step-2 failure) and the restated Universal-Algorithm falsifier — go to the author. Both are one-line edits to v5.1 text and neither touches the base code.

## Forcing Test
- Test A (Ground): grounded on the instrument side (the algorithm's text is fixed and was executed literally) and on the reality side (five fetched or sweep-cited measurements, four of them from primary sources retrieved this run). Not a ground question in v5.3's sense — it is a within-system audit of an instrument.
- Test B (Uniqueness): three candidates entered for "why canon's falsifiers are non-operational". (i) *The algorithm is badly designed.* **Forced-no**: run 1 produced a fired falsifier and a revision; runs 2 and 3 produced correct diagnoses. (ii) *The register rows were written without running the algorithm.* Survives — every defect found sits in a register row's wording, and step 2 catches each in one pass. (iii) *Step 6's status vocabulary is incomplete.* Survives, and is narrower than (ii): it explains why a non-claim can be filed as "Open" without anyone noticing. (ii) and (iii) are compatible and both survive; not unique.
- Test C (Direction): the algorithm runs claim → falsifier → evidence → status. Canon's register runs status → evidence → falsifier: each row's "Status" column is confident and the "What Would Disprove It" column is back-filled to match. That is the arrow reversed, and it is the mechanism of (ii). Direction discriminates the two survivors: (ii) is a process failure, (iii) a design gap, and both are present.
- Test D (Falsifiability): falsifier stated at step 3 of the Scientific Inquiry run — *find a sharp canonical claim about a cell for which step 2 cannot produce an experiment.* Run 1 is such a claim and step 2 produced one. **Falsifier did not fire.** Conversely the falsifier for "the register is operational" — *find one register row whose step-2 output is an experiment* — was tested on two rows and fired on neither.
- **Outcome:** `stipulation` for canon's claim that Layer ◇ makes the framework falsifiable: as written, the register is a presentation of rigour rather than an executed instrument. The algorithm itself is not stipulation — it is the thing that proves the register is. `diff_state: accounted` records that reality's falsifiable content about a cell and canon's instrument are consistent; the gap is at the register, already filed as F-042/F-043/F-085 and not re-counted here.

## Anti-Operation (the gap this opens)

- **Canon has no fifth status.** Step 6 offers resolved / bounded / held open / refuted. A claim that fails step 2 is none of these, and folding it into "held open" destroys the distinction the Variable Principle exists to protect: an honestly held-open variable and an unfalsifiable assertion are then indistinguishable in the register. **New gap: canon needs a `not-a-claim` status, and a rule that it may not be recorded as held-open.** This bears on every audit record in this repository, because `intake_result: open` inherits the same ambiguity.
- **No rule says who re-runs the register.** The algorithm exists, the register exists, and nothing in canon requires the second to be produced by the first. A register row can survive indefinitely without ever being executed. What procedure re-runs ◇ on its own rows, and on what cadence?
- **A fired falsifier has no stated cost.** Run 1 refuted "universal" and canon has no rule for what one refutation costs a claim that was filed "Bounded. High confidence." (the same missing rule F-073 reached from the Correspondence side).
- **The instrument was tested on three claims, not seventy.** Two of three register rows tested failed step 2. Whether the rate is 2/3 or higher across all thirteen rows is unmeasured here.
