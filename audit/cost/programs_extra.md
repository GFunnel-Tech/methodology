# Program cost record: additional programs (Reality Audit)

A sourced record of what the 17 programs in [`new_programs.json`](new_programs.json) cost. It follows the rules of [`facilities.md`](facilities.md). Every figure comes from a page fetched on **2026-09-27** (curl or WebFetch); none is from memory. Where no figure fitting the requested scope could be fetched, the item is marked **held-open** (Variable Principle). A held-open item may still carry partial or container figures, labelled as such.

Machine-readable version: [`programs_extra.json`](programs_extra.json), in the same schema as `facilities.json`, with `id` set to the slug. A figure carries its own `source_url` / `source_type` when these differ from the program's main source.

Conventions: amounts are as the source states them. **Price year** is shown only when the source states it; `—` means not stated, usually then-year or nominal. For annual budget lines, the fiscal year is used as the price year. "Primary" means an agency, operator, audit, funder-database or collaboration document. "Secondary" means press or encyclopedia. These figures are **not** yet wired into `compute_cost.py`.

## Held open

- **KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes)** (`kegg`): No currency amount found. The operator's own page (fetched) gives only ratios: 'public funding accounts for only about one tenth of our total operational cost', and that an earlier NBDC (JST) grant 'amounts to only one-third of the entire operational cost'. It says KEGG was fully grant-funded (JST BIRD, then NBDC) until its 16th year. Tried and failed: KAKEN grant database (kaken.nii.ac.jp returned a robot check), GenomeWeb article on the 2011 subscription move (403). Held open: absolute annual operating cost and 1995-2026 cumulative cost.
- **BICEP2 / Keck Array / BICEP3 (South Pole CMB polarization)** (`bicep-keck`): Derivation: the BK18 paper (arXiv:2110.00483, fetched) acknowledgments name 11 NSF grants: 0742818, 0742592, 1044978, 1110087, 1145172, 1145143, 1145248, 1639040, 1638957, 1638978 & 1638970. Each amount was fetched from the NSF Award API (api.nsf.gov). Sum of obligations = $23,941,742 (nominal; some awards run past 2021, so this overstates the 'to BK18' NSF share). NOT included, amounts not found: Keck Foundation, NASA detector grants (06-ARPA206-0040, 10-SAT10-0017, 12-SAT12-0031, 14-SAT14-0009, 16-SAT-16-0002), JPL R&TD, Moore Foundation, CFI (UBC), UK STFC, DOE (Stanford/SLAC), and shared South Pole Station / US Antarctic Program logistics (explicitly excluded here, not costed). A construction + operations total is held open.
- **Casgevy (exa-cel) development and clinical program (Vertex / CRISPR Therapeutics)** (`exa-cel-clinical-program`): Total R&D + clinical-trial spend to FDA approval (Dec 2023) is not disclosed. Neither Vertex nor CRISPR Therapeutics reports a program total. These are CRISPR Therapeutics' annual 'collaboration expense, net' for the CASGEVY program from its FY2023 10-K (SEC EDGAR, fetched). Under the amended collaboration (2021) Vertex leads and holds 60% of program economics, CRISPR 40%. The 10-K notes the Company 'deferred $80.9 million and $36.1 million of its share of costs' for 2023 and 2022. Derivation, not a sourced total: dividing CRISPR's 40% share by 0.4 would give a rough program cost for 2021-2023, but that is not done here because the 60/40 split and the deferral treatment make it an assumption. Program costs before 2021 (the 2015-2020 co-development under the original 50/50 JDA) and Vertex's own R&D line for exa-cel are held open. Transfers between the companies ($900M up-front in 2021, $200M approval milestone) are deal payments, not development costs, and are not recorded as cost.
- **MEG / MEG II (PSI, mu -> e gamma search)** (`meg`): No cost figure found. Tried: MEG proposal documents on meg.web.psi.ch (PSI R-99-05 and the INFN proposal PDFs return a JavaScript/robot challenge page, not the PDF); arXiv MEG detector and MEG II design papers (search snippets show no cost); PSI media pages; SNSF data portal. The SNSF grant cited in MEG II papers (137738, 'Fundamentale Myonenphysik am Paul Scherrer Institut', 2013-2015) shows an approved amount of about CHF 0.13M in a WebFetch rendering. That is a single small group grant, not the detector or beam cost, so it is not recorded as a figure. Detector construction (INFN, ICEPP/Tokyo, PSI, UC Irvine, JINR) and PSI beam-time costs are held open.
- **MICROSCOPE (CNES equivalence-principle satellite)** (`microscope`): Mission lifecycle cost held open. Fetched with no cost figure: cnes.fr/en/projects/microscope, eoPortal MicroSCOPE page, en/fr Wikipedia. - **NCBI (US National Center for Biotechnology Information)** (`ncbi`): The NLM congressional justifications (FY2016, FY2020, FY2021, FY2025; fetched from nlm.nih.gov) do not break out an NCBI-only budget line. NCBI sits inside 'Intramural Programs' (FY2023 Final $399.265M; table in $ thousands; columns FY2023 Final / FY2024 CR / FY2025 PB). NCBI's annual budget, and the share spent maintaining genetic-code tables (tiny, not separable), are held open. The genetic code itself was established by 1960s experiments; NCBI only curates the translation tables.
- **NICER (ISS X-ray timing instrument)** (`nicer`): Actual NICER development cost is held open. - **UN World Urbanization Prospects (UN DESA Population Division; via World Bank WDI)** (`un-wup`): Source: UN proposed programme budget for 2026, A/80/6 (Sect. 9) Economic and social affairs (fetched via documents.un.org). - **US Census Bureau Business Formation Statistics** (`us-census-bfs`): No budget line found. Census funds BFS inside the Current Economic Statistics / business statistics programs, so even a fetched CJ would likely give only a container line. Program operating cost to 2026 is held open.

## Disagreements between sources

- **ALMA construction:** $1.4 B (ESO eso1312; ALMA factsheet) against $1.3 B (NRAO inauguration release, same event). Neither states a price year.
- **MESSENGER:** $427 M (APL/NASA launch press kit, 2004, primary mission incl. operations) against $446 M (Spaceflight Now, 2015) against "under US$450 million" (Wikipedia). The later figures probably include the extended missions, but no source says so.

These are different scopes and price levels, not true conflicts.
