## §1 · The frame

**Stability is a property of the container, not of the part.** The fold is the content. The container is the relation that holds the content *off its specific point* (v5.4 §3). Inside the container the content is not at rest: it is a superposition that samples every outcome the container admits. The container persists while none of the sampled outcomes is an exit.

| Term | Under this frame |
| --- | --- |
| Container | the boundary that fixes which outcomes are admitted (a frequency filter, v5.4 §4a.4 #5) |
| Fold / contained | the superposition of admitted outcomes |
| Stability | no admitted outcome is an exit (no accessible specific point) |
| Specific point (§3) | the one admitted outcome that is an exit |
| Lifetime | time until the sampling lands on the exit — `τ = 1/(2π·Δf)` (§4a.2) |
| Tunneling | the exit is admitted at low amplitude |
| Decoherence | the container leaks: an outside node reads outcomes, one per node (§4a.4 #7) |
| Collapse to a lower state (Stage 3) | the container closes on one outcome and releases the rest as a ripple |

## §3 · What this does to v5.4's constructs

**Lamina — proposed demotion to a reading, not a construct.** Folding is containment read from inside; containment is folding read from outside (node-reading rule). One relation, two nodes. A lamina is what a container looks like to a node inside it. Nothing in §2 needs "lamina" that "container" does not already supply.

- An earlier in-session claim that the atom shows *two container classes* (fold vs mode) is **retracted** here: it is one relation at two coupling strengths, not two kinds.
- The lamina falsifier (v5.4 §5, §14 #3) survives the demotion and is now cheaper to run: shell-closure plateaus in binding per nucleon are discrete, measured, and many; saturation density is one number.

**⊙ and KC — the same relation at the Third Kingdom.** The Forcing Test holds every candidate (live hypotheses) inside the methodology's container until one outcome forces; a knowledge container with its open edge is a superposition held in writing. Correspondence, labeled derivation.
